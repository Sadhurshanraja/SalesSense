THE PROBLEM
The Core Problem
Small-scale specialty spice retailers lack reliable, data-driven forecasting solutions, leading to costly inventory mismanagement (overstocking perishable goods or understocking resulting in missed sales).
Failure of Traditional Methods
"Traditional models frequently yield low explanatory power for complex retail dynamics." (Ganguly & Mukherjee, 2024)
Relying on manual estimation and basic statistical models fails to capture complex, seasonal, and non-linear sales patterns.

Generalized Data
Existing studies rely on massive datasets (Walmart), overlooking constraints like perishability in niche spice retail.
Operational Gap
Prior research neglects integrating high-accuracy predictions into daily operational planning (Venkiteela, 2025).
Interpretability
High-performing models like XGBoost are often "Black-Boxes", reducing manager trust (Davinder Pal Singh, 2023).

MODEL PIPELINE & DATA
Dataset & ETL
Source: Proprietary unstructured records from a spice shop.Size: 43 monthly records (April 2022 – Jan 2026). Split 40:3.Custom ETL: Parser with regex and dynamic header detection to clean inconsistent Excel bookkeeping files.
Feature Engineering
Short-term: Generated Lag1, Lag2, Lag3.Trends: RollingMean3, RollingStd3.Seasonality: MonthNum, MonthOfYear features.
Model & Training
Algorithm: XGBoost selected for superior performance on small tabular data over LSTMs.Tuning: Strict regularization (LR=0.05, Depth=3) to prevent overfitting to SME noise.
Experiments
Comparative: RF, Linear Regression, and LSTM tested before finalizing XGBoost.Real-time Architecture: FastAPI asynchronous state management for on-the-fly training upon data upload.
