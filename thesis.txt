ML-Based Sales Forecasting for Retail Spices  
ABSTRACT  
 
This project addresses the challenge of inaccurate sales forecasting in small -scale retail 
businesses, particularly in niche domains such as spice retailing, where poor predictions lead 
to inefficient inventory management and suboptimal decision -making. Th e study proposes a 
machine learning –based approach using the XGBoost regression model to generate accurate 
six-month sales forecasts from historical data.To improve model reliability, time -based and 
lag features are engineered to capture seasonal patterns and temporal dependencies. The 
system further incorporates SHAP -based interpretability to explain model predictions and 
enhance transparency for non -technical users. In addition, a scenario -based component is 
included to allow users to adjust business para meters and explore different forecasting 
outcomes.The results demonstrate that the proposed approach provides a practical and 
interpretable solution for supporting inventory planning and data -driven decision -making in 
small retail environments.

1.2 Problem Background  
 
1.2.1 Challenges Faced by Small Retail Businesses  
Small retail businesses, particularly those operating in sectors such as specialty food 
ingredients and spices, face a dynamic and unpredictable environment. One of the primary 
challenges is aligning critical operational functions such as inventory managem ent, financial 
planning, and resource allocation with ever -changing consumer demand.  
• Inaccurate demand forecasting often leads to two costly outcomes: Overstocking, 
which ties up capital in slow -moving or perishable goods and increases holding costs.  
• Understocking, which results in missed sales opportunities, reduced customer 
satisfaction, and potential long -term brand damage.  
For a specialty spice shop, these challenges are intensified by a variety of influencing factors 
including seasonal demand, cultural festivals, holiday cooking trends, and promotional 
activities. Traditional forecasting methods that rely on simple averages  or basic regression 
fail to capture these complex, non -linear market dynamics. Consequently, small business 
owners are often forced to make reactive decisions rather than proactive, data -driven 
strategies.

20222296                                                            10 1.2.2 Limitations of Traditional Forecasting Techniques  
Historically, sales forecasting in small and medium -sized enterprises (SMEs) has relied 
heavily on basic statistical models such as Linear Regression or Moving Averages. While 
these techniques provide a general understanding of trends, they often underperf orm in real -
world retail contexts where sales data is non -linear, high -dimensional, and influenced by 
multiple factors.  
Studies have shown that such models frequently yield low explanatory power, indicating their 
limited ability to accurately represent com plex patterns in retail data (Ganguly & Mukherjee, 
2024). As a result, businesses relying solely on traditional methods suffer from inefficient 
inventory control, budget mismanagement, and financial uncertainty.  
The rapid evolution of digital commerce, coupled with an abundance of transactional and 
financial data, highlights the urgent need for advanced analytical methods capable of 
handling these intricate relationships.  
 
1.2.3 The Need for Machine Learning -Based Forecasting  
The advancement of machine learning (ML) has significantly improved the capability of 
forecasting systems to handle complex and non -linear data patterns. Unlike traditional 
statistical approaches, ML models such as Random Forest, XGBoost, and Long Short -Term 
Memory (LSTM) are capable of capturing hidden relationships within time series data, 
making them more suitable for retail sales prediction.  
For a small -scale spice retail business, the application of ML enables more accurate multi -
step-ahead forecasting, particularly for predicting sales over a six -month horizon. These 
predictions provide valuable support for key business decisions, including inventory planning 
and demand management.  
In this project, the forecasting output is further extended through a scenario -based approach, 
where users can adjust business parameters such as estimated cost or demand variations. This 
allows the system to provide flexible, what -if analysis rather than fixed financial predictions.  
By integrating machine learning –based forecasting with user -driven inputs and interpretable 
outputs, small business owners can shift from reactive decision -making to more informed and 
data-driven strategies.

The core problem addressed in this research is the lack of a reliable and data -driven 
forecasting solution for small -scale retail businesses, particularly in niche domains such as 
spice retailing. Many small retailers rely on manual estimation or basic sta tistical methods,

The challenge of this research is to design and develop a machine learning –based system 
capable of generating accurate six -month sales forecasts for a small -scale spice retail business 
using historical sales data. The system should not only improve predict ion accuracy through 
models such as XGBoost but also provide interpretable and practical outputs that support 
inventory planning and scenario -based business decision -making.  
 
1.4 Research Motivation  
The motivation behind this research arises from the practical challenges faced by small -scale 
retail businesses in accurately predicting future sales and managing inventory effectively. In 
niche domains such as spice retailing, demand is highly influenced by seasonal patterns, 
cultural events, and changing customer behavior, making traditional forecasting methods 
unreliable. This often leads to inefficient stock management and missed business 
opportunities.From a technical perspective, the project is motiva ted by the need to apply 
advanced machine learning techniques, particularly XGBoost, to address the complexity of 
multi -step time series forecasting. Predicting sales over a six -month horizon requires capturing 
non-linear patterns and temporal dependencies  within limited historical data, which presents a 
significant challenge.In addition, there is a need to move beyond pure prediction and provide 
outputs that are understandable and usable by non -technical users. Therefore, this project is 
further motivated by the goal of combining accurate forecasting with interpretable insights and 
simple scenario -based analysis, enabling small business owners to make more informed and 
practical decisions.

20222296                                                            13 1.6 Research Gap  
Despite significant advancements in machine learning –based sales forecasting, several key 
research gaps persist that justify the need for this project.  
First, most existing studies rely on generalized datasets from large retail chains such as 
Walmart or BigMart, overlooking the specific challenges of specialized retail sectors. There 
remains limited exploration of niche markets like food ingredients or sp ices, where sales 
patterns, product perishability, and seasonal demand introduce unique forecasting 
complexities (Davinder Pal Singh, 2023).  
Secondly, prior research has largely focused on improving predictive accuracy while 
neglecting the operational and managerial usability of model outputs. Many studies conclude 
at the evaluation stage (using metrics such as RMSE or MAE), without addressing how such 
models can be integrated into day -to-day business processes like inventory planning and 
decision support (Padmanabhan Venkiteela, 2025).  
In addition, existing approaches rarely incorporate user -adjustable business parameters, 
limiting the flexibility of models in real -world scenarios. Small business owners often require 
the ability to explore different “what -if” conditions, such as changes in demand or cost 
assumptions, which is not sufficiently addressed in current research.  
Finally, high -performing algorithms such as XGBoost often suffer from limited 
interpretability, creating a “black -box” perception that reduces managerial trust and hinders 
strategic decision -making. A clear need exists for approaches that not only achieve high 
accuracy but also enhance transparency and explainability in model outcomes (Davinder Pal 
Singh, 2023).  
To address these gaps, this project proposes the development of an interpretable and 
application -oriented XGBoost -based forecasting system tailored to the spice retail domain. 
The system integrates SHAP (SHapley Additive Explanations) to visualize feature 
contributions and incorporates scenario -based analysis to improve practical usability, thereby 
contributing to both the practical and academic dimensions of predictive analytics.

1.8 Research Challenges  
This project will address several inherent research challenges:  
1. Data Scarcity and Quality: Ensuring the developed models can generate effective forecasts 
with potentially limited or sparse historical sales data (a common SME constraint) while 
maintaining high accuracy, as model performance is heavily reliant on data  quality.  
2. Computational Resources: The training and optimization (hyperparameter tuning) of 
complex ML models necessitate sufficient computational power, which must be addressed, 
potentially by leveraging cloud platforms or robust local hardware.  
3. Model Selection and Optimization: Identifying the single best -performing model or 
optimizing a robust hybrid strategy capable of balancing the capture of linear trends, non -
linear patterns, and significant seasonality over the challenging 6 -month foreca sting horizon.  
4. Integration of Forecasts: Developing a robust and validated methodology to translate a 
statistical forecast into dynamic parameters for financial and inventory formulas requires a 
careful synthesis of data science and business management principles.

2.2 Problem Domain  
Accurate sales forecasting is a cornerstone of retail operations. In the modern digital age, the 
conventional approach to business analysis has been transformed by advancements in 
machine learning and predictive analytics (Ahaggach, Abrouk and Lebon, 2024) . ML 
techniques allow organizations to analyze historical data  to identify patterns and trends, 
generating accurate prognoses relating to future activities. This is particularly critical in 
competitive markets where profit margins are pressured (Ganguly & Mukherjee, 2024).  
2.2.1 The Challenge of Retail Sales as a Time Series Problem  
Sales forecasting is fundamentally approached as a time series problem. Time series 
forecasting consists of predicting several future observations of a given sequence of historical 
data. In retail environments, sales data are often noisy and non -stationary , exhibiting complex 
trends, seasonal cycles, and volatilities that change over time (Ahaggach, Abrouk and Lebon, 
2024).  
Historically, forecasting relied on statistical methods, but these often fall short when dealing 
with the non -linear relationships and complexity inherent in sales dynamics. For instance, 
Linear Regression (LR) models often exhibit suboptimal performance, with some studies 
finding $R^2$ values around $0.5$, indicating that only about half of the variability in sales 
was explained by the linear model. This inadequacy underscores the necessity for more 
robust, non -linear models capable of handling intricate s ales dynamics (Ganguly & 
Mukherjee, 2024; Fredén & Larsson, 2020)  
Furthermore, the specific objective of predicting sales six months ahead constitutes a multi -
step-ahead forecasting problem. This challenge requires careful selection of algorithms and 
strategies (such as recursive or direct forecasting methods) to manage complexity and 
maintain accuracy over a long forecast horizon (Souhaib Ben Taie,2014).

The progression of research in sales forecasting has transitioned from relying on simpler 
statistical models to embracing complex, powerful machine learning (ML) and deep learning 
(DL) algorithms. This shift has been driven by the need to handle the increa sing volume and

1. Singh (2023)  
This study, focusing on the BigMart Sales dataset, aimed to predict sales patterns and 
consumer behaviour using machine learning techniques in the business sector. The primary 
methodology involved a comparative classification study, applying and evaluating  models 
such as XGBoost, K -Nearest Neighbours (KNN), Generalized Linear Model (GLM), and 
Decision Tree. A comprehensive pipeline was followed, including data preprocessing steps 
like feature creation, feature reduction via PCA (Principal Component Analysis ), and 
handling missing values. The core contribution was demonstrating the predictive capacity of 
KNN, with an "upgraded KNN customer categorisation model" achieving an impressive 
accuracy greater than 0.925. However, the study’s limitation lies in its pr imary focus on 
classification  (predicting product categories or behaviour) rather than the regression  task of 
predicting precise sales values, which is essential for accurate inventory ordering and 
financial reporting in your food ingredients shop.  
2. Ganguly & Mukherjee (2024)  
This research addressed the imperative of improving retail sales prediction accuracy by 
rigorously optimizing various ML models to navigate complex challenges like seasonality 
and numerous product families. The authors utilized a dataset provided by Favori ta Stores, a 
retail chain in Ecuador. Their methodology centered on ensemble methods and meticulous 
optimization techniques, comparing models such as Linear Regression (LR), Random Forest 
(RF), Gradient Boosting (GB), and XGBoost. The core contribution was  the achievement of 
significantly improved results, where an optimized Random Forest (RF) model was able to 
explain 94.5% of the variance in sales ($R^2$ of 0.945). The critique highlights the 
inadequacy of simpler approaches, noting that initial LR applic ation showed overall 
suboptimal performance, with adjusted $R^2$ values frequently stabilizing around 0.5. This 
necessitates the use of complex, computationally intensive methods like optimized RF to 
achieve real -world viability.  
3. Ben Taieb (2014)  
The primary objective of this doctoral work was to contribute to the development of robust 
multi -step-ahead forecasting strategies based on machine learning algorithms. This is directly 
relevant to your 6 -month prediction objective. The study systematicall y compared traditional 
forecasting approaches, specifically analysing the difference between recursive (iterating a 
one-step-ahead model) and direct (a separate model for each horizon) strategies. The key 
contribution involved the introduction of multi -stage forecasting strategies —such as rectify 
and boost strategies —designed to address the difficult task of choosing between recursive 
and direct forecasts by combining the best properties of both. The analysis relied on 
decomposing the Mean Squared Forecast Errors (MSE) into its bias and variance 
components, a rigorous methodology for evaluating error sources over longer horizons. A 
persistent limitation noted in the broader literature is the limited attention given to multi -step

This systematic mapping study (SMS) provided a comprehensive overview of the sales 
forecasting landscape between 2013 and 2024, meticulously reviewing over 1,000 papers. 
The research objective was to construct a defined taxonomy of technologies used, scrut inize

contribution lies in its synthesis of trends, confirming that ML algorithms, statistical methods,

prediction specifically for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs). The motivation stemmed 
from the critical observation that SMEs often rely on rudimentary computational or manua l 
methods, and existing sophisticated forecasting models are often too complex and expensive 
for them. The methodology proposed the use of XGBoost and features such as Cyclic 
Encoding to capture seasonal variations. The main contribution is the development  of a low -
cost, easy -to-use predictive inventory system tailored for SMEs, which focuses on stock 
balancing to avoid overstocking and understocking. Critically, this research implies that the

small businesses over potentially achieving marginal gains in accuracy from highly complex, 
resource -intensive models designed for large enterprises.

models, including ARIMA, the deep learning technique LSTM (Long Short -Term Memory)

from these models to establish which is most suitable for time -series forecasting in a retail 
context. The comparative models employed, such as ARIMA, require that the model be fitted 
numerous times for each identifier's distinctive value du e to the algorithm's adaptability. This 
highlights a limitation related to computational efficiency and scalability when dealing with a 
high number of distinct products.

       FOR EACH Row in file DO:

                   Add {Date, Product, Quantity, Value} to `CleanedData`  
               END FOR  
           END IF  
       END FOR  
   END FOR  
   
   RETURN `CleanedData`

This section is emphasized to show the technical challenges experienced in the process of 
creating SalesSense and the engineering solutions that were adopted to address them.  
 
7.5.1 Quality of Data and Inconsistency.  
Challenge: Unstructured nature of the legacy data was the major challenge. All the source 
files were the manually created Excel books of books (files/*/.xlsx) that were not made by 
machines.  
Problems: Combined rows, disparate date headers (e.g. one row says For April 2024 and 
another April -24), and the rows with Particulars showing up with varying indices.  
Impact: Standard library functions (such as pd.read_excel) were not able to read the tables, 
and empty or misaligned datasets were obtained.  
Solution: We adopted a home made Robust ETL Parser in data_processor.py  
Dynamic Header Detection: The script does not predetermine the row number on which it 
will look at the headers but scans the first 50 rows to find the keyword Particulars.  
Regex Cleaning: A clean_value Regular Expression (re.search(r"([ \d.]+), s)) is a function 
created to take a numerical value of a string such as 10 kgs or 1,200.00 and is capable of 
overcoming the error of human formatting.  
 
7.5.2 Overfitting of Model to Small Dataset.  
Difficulty: The dataset used was monthly data in a limited number of years. Deep Learning 
models (such as LSTMs) generally need thousands of observations to be generalized.  
Impact: Early work with complex neural networks achieved poor performance as the model 
overfitted, that is, it remembered the training data but could not make accurate predictions of 
future trends.  
Rationale: XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting) was chosen over Deep Neural Network.  
Why: XGBoost is an ensemble algorithm (a decision -tree) which works remarkably well with 
a small, structured tabular data.  
Regularization: To ensure that the model does not get too complicated, tuned 
hyperparameters (learning rate 0.05, max depth=3) so that the model only learns the overall 
trend and not noise.

Evaluation Metrics  
1.Mean Absolute Error (MAE)  
MAE measures the average absolute difference between actual and predicted values.  
2.Root Mean Square Error (RMSE)  
RMSE measures the square root of the average squared differences between predicted 
and actual values. It penalizes larger errors more heavily.  
3.Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE)  
MAPE represents the prediction error as a percentage.  
4.R-squared (R² Score)  
R² indicates how well the model explains the variance in the data.
